## Thematic Breakdown of Relationships

### 1. Architectural Influence & Precursors (8 Edges)
These represent major architectural evolutionary milestones across Indian heritage:
- **Humayun's Tomb $\rightarrow$ Taj Mahal (`architectural_influence`)**: Introduction of the bulbous double dome and radial charbagh plan directly prefiguring the Taj Mahal.
- **Itimad-ud-Daulah $\rightarrow$ Taj Mahal (`architectural_influence`)**: Transition from red sandstone to pure white marble with polychrome pietra dura (parchin kari), widely recognized as the 'Baby Taj'.
- **Akbar's Tomb (Sikandra) $\rightarrow$ Taj Mahal (`architectural_influence`)**: The South Gate at Sikandra was the first to use four detached white marble minarets, inspiring the placement of four corner minarets on the Taj Mahal terrace.
- **Humayun's Tomb $\rightarrow$ Safdarjung's Tomb (`architectural_influence`)**: Safdarjung's Tomb represents the last grand enclosed garden tomb in Delhi, deliberately designed in the charbagh tradition of Humayun's Tomb.
- **Lodhi Gardens $\rightarrow$ Humayun's Tomb (`architectural_influence`)**: Sikandar Lodi's tomb was the earliest octagonal garden tomb with an embryonic double dome in Delhi, directly informing Humayun's Tomb.
- **Fatehpur Sikri $\rightarrow$ Jama Masjid (`architectural_influence`)**: Grand congregational courtyard and gateway prototypes from Fatehpur Sikri adapted in Shah Jahan's Friday Mosque in Delhi.
- **Mehrauli Archaeological Park $\rightarrow$ Lodhi Gardens (`architectural_influence`)**: Early true arches and dome construction from Balban's Tomb and Jamali Kamali evolving into high Sultanate tomb design.
- **Lodhi Gardens $\rightarrow$ Safdarjung's Tomb (`successor_of`)**: Contiguous historic funerary progression across Lodhi Road, spanning Sultanate to late Mughal tomb design.

### 2. Imperial Capitals & Architectural Succession (6 Edges)
These capture the movement of imperial seats and urban centers:
- **Agra Fort $\rightarrow$ Red Fort (`capital_relationship`)**: Shah Jahan transferred the Mughal capital from Agra Fort to the newly founded Shahjahanabad (Red Fort) in 1648.
- **Qutb Complex $\rightarrow$ Tughlaqabad Fort (`successor_of`)**: Ghiyas-ud-din Tughlaq founded the third historic city of Delhi, succeeding the early Sultanate capital at Qutb/Mehrauli.
- **Purana Qila $\rightarrow$ Qutb Complex (`successor_of`)**: Dinpanah / Shergarh as the sixth historic city of Delhi continuing the imperial succession begun at the first city.
- **Purana Qila $\rightarrow$ Lodhi Gardens (`successor_of`)**: Suri and early Mughal building at Purana Qila following the overthrow of the Lodi dynasty interred at Lodhi Gardens.
- **Akbar's Tomb $\rightarrow$ Agra Fort (`successor_of`)**: Jahangir governed from Agra Fort and oversaw the completion of his father Akbar's funerary complex at Sikandra (1613).
- **Lodhi Gardens $\rightarrow$ Qutb Complex (`same_dynasty`)**: Final dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate (Sayyid & Lodi) ruling in succession to the early Mamluk/Khalji Sultanate at Qutb.

### 3. Shared Imperial Patronage & Dynastic Governance (7 Edges)
These represent documented shared patronage by specific emperors and prime ministers:
- **Agra Fort $\rightarrow$ Taj Mahal (`same_patron`)**: Both commissioned and adorned in white marble by Shah Jahan.
- **Agra Fort $\rightarrow$ Fatehpur Sikri (`same_patron`)**: Both executed in red sandstone under Emperor Akbar.
- **Red Fort $\rightarrow$ Jama Masjid (`same_patron`)**: Commissioned by Shah Jahan as civic and religious focal points of Shahjahanabad.
- **Akbar's Tomb $\rightarrow$ Fatehpur Sikri (`same_dynasty`)**: Planned by Akbar, founder of Fatehpur Sikri, and finished by Jahangir.
- **Itimad-ud-Daulah $\rightarrow$ Akbar's Tomb (`same_dynasty`)**: Both erected in Agra during Jahangir's reign.
- **Red Fort $\rightarrow$ Safdarjung's Tomb (`same_dynasty`)**: Safdarjung served as Grand Vizier of the Mughal Empire under Ahmad Shah Bahadur at the Red Fort.
- **Itimad-ud-Daulah $\rightarrow$ Agra Fort (`burial_association`)**: Mirza Ghiyas Beg was chief minister to Jahangir at Agra Fort, and his daughter Empress Nur Jahan built his mausoleum.

### 4. Significant Historical Events & Dynastic Associations (5 Edges)
- **Purana Qila $\rightarrow$ Humayun's Tomb (`historical_event`)**: Humayun died at Purana Qila in 1556 after falling from the Sher Mandal library; entombed at Humayun's Tomb nearby.
- **Red Fort $\rightarrow$ Humayun's Tomb (`historical_event`)**: Bahadur Shah Zafar fled the Red Fort in 1857 and took refuge at Humayun's Tomb where he was captured.
- **Red Fort $\rightarrow$ Jantar Mantar (`historical_event`)**: Maharaja Jai Singh II built Jantar Mantar under imperial commission by Mughal Emperor Muhammad Shah ruling from Red Fort.
- **Qutb Complex $\rightarrow$ Mehrauli Archaeological Park (`historical_event`)**: Contiguous historic precinct representing the first fortified city of Delhi (Lal Kot / Qila Rai Pithora).
- **Agra Fort $\rightarrow$ Akbar's Tomb (`capital_relationship`)**: Imperial burial situated along the northwestern royal road connecting Agra Fort to Delhi.
